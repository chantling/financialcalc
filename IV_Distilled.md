# Intrinsic Value Calculation: The Distilled Best Approach

## Executive Summary

Based on analysis of 14 comprehensive video summaries from leading value investing educators, this guide synthesizes the most robust and practical approach to calculating intrinsic value. The distilled methodology combines the strengths of multiple approaches while avoiding their individual weaknesses.

---

## Part 1: Fundamental Principles (Universal Consensus)

### 1.1 The Core Definition

**Warren Buffett's Definition (universally cited):**
"Intrinsic value is the present value of all the cash that a business will generate from now to Judgment Day, discounted at the proper discount rate."

**The Three Essential Questions (Buffett's Framework):**
1. How much cash will the business return to you?
2. When will you receive that cash?
3. How certain are you of receiving it?

### 1.2 The Investment Reality

**All presenters agree:**
- Intrinsic value is NOT an exact number—it's an estimate, a range
- The goal is to be "approximately right" rather than "precisely wrong"
- Future is impossible to predict with certainty
- Different reasonable investors will calculate different values for the same company
- No valuation method is perfect or 100% accurate

**Universal agreement on purpose:**
- Prevent overpaying for stocks
- Identify undervalued opportunities
- Provide a framework for rational investment decisions
- Create margin of safety against estimation errors

## Part 2: Commonalities Between Presenters

### 2.1 Universal Methodological Components

**All 14 presenters agree on these core elements:**

1. **Cash Flow Focus:** Every method centers on cash flows, not earnings alone
   - Free cash flow is the preferred metric
   - Operating cash flow minus capital expenditures
   - Some discuss "owner's earnings" (preferred by Buffett)

2. **Growth Rate Estimation:** All require projecting future growth
   - Historical performance is the starting point
   - Conservative assumptions are universally recommended
   - Growth rates significantly impact valuation results

3. **Discounting Future Values:** All methods use time value of money
   - Future cash flows must be discounted to present value
   - The concept is universally applied

4. **Terminal Value:** All account for value beyond projection period
   - Using multiples (P/E, P/FCF) is the standard approach
   - Represents selling the business at end of projection period

5. **Margin of Safety:** Every presenter emphasizes this as critical
   - Range of 10-50% margin discussed
   - More conservative investors prefer larger margins
   - Essential protection against errors

### 2.2 Universal Principles

**Philosophical agreements across all presenters:**

1. **Business Understanding Precedes Valuation**
   - Never calculate intrinsic value for businesses you don't understand
   - Qualitative analysis is as important as quantitative
   - 95% of businesses are "too hard" (Buffett)

2. **Consistency Over Complexity**
   - Use consistent methodology across all valuations
   - Simplicity is preferred over theoretical precision
   - Enables fair comparisons between investments

3. **Patience and Discipline**
   - Wait for market to present opportunities
   - Don't chase stocks
   - Maintain watchlists and be patient

4. **Market vs. Value Distinction**
   - Price ≠ Value
   - Price fluctuates daily; value changes more slowly
   - Market sentiment can drive prices far from intrinsic value

## Part 3: Differences Between Presenters

### 3.1 Methodological Differences

**Three Primary Approaches Identified:**

#### Approach A: Full Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) Model
- **Presenters:** Sven Carlin (2 videos), New Money, Brent Coleman, Investing with Tom, Cooper Academy
- **Complexity:** High
- **Components:**
  - Project cash flows year-by-year (typically 10 years)
  - Often use two-period growth structure (Years 1-5, Years 6-10)
  - Discount each year's cash flow individually
  - Calculate terminal value separately
  - Sum all present values

#### Approach B: Benjamin Graham Formula
- **Presenters:** Dividendology
- **Complexity:** Low
- **Formula:** EPS × [8.5 + (2 × G)] × 4.4 ÷ Y
  - EPS = Earnings Per Share
  - G = Expected growth rate (next 5 years)
  - Y = Current AAA corporate bond yield
- **Variation:** Revised formula uses [7 + (1 × G)] for more conservative valuation

#### Approach C: Simplified P/E-Growth Method
- **Presenters:** InvestingPH (2 videos), Invest with Wesley
- **Complexity:** Medium
- **Process:**
  - Calculate future intrinsic value (EPS grown × P/E multiple)
  - Discount to present value
  - Apply margin of safety
  - Does not require year-by-year cash flow projection

### 3.2 Specific Component Differences

**1. Discount Rate Selection:**

| Approach | Method | Proponents |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Consistent Personal Return | Use fixed rate (10%) for all companies | Sven Carlin, Investing with Tom, Cooper Academy, New Money |
| Market Historical | Use S&P 500 average (10-12%) | Brent Coleman, Everything Money |
| Risk-Free Rate | Use Treasury rates | Some theoretical discussions |
| WACC/Complex | Weighted Average Cost of Capital | Academic approaches (rarely used) |

**Consensus recommendation:** Use consistent rate (10%) for all valuations to enable fair comparison

**2. Growth Rate Estimation:**

| Method | Description | Proponents |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| Historical Average | Simple average of past growth | Multiple |
| Compounded Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) | More accurate, excludes outliers | InvestingPH, Brent Coleman |
| Analyst Projections | Use analyst estimates | Investing with Tom |
| Conservative Adjustment | Historical rate reduced by 20-50% | Most presenters |
| Sustainable Growth Rate | ROE × retention ratio | InvestingPH (mentioned) |

**Consensus recommendation:** Use conservative estimates based on historical performance, preferably CAGR

**3. Terminal Value Multiple:**

| Range | Business Quality | Proponents |
|-------|----------------|------------|
| 10x | Poor/average quality | Investing with Tom |
| 12-15x | Good quality | Most presenters |
| 15-20x | High quality | Investing with Tom, Brent Coleman |
| 20-30x+ | Exceptional growth | Some presenters (rare) |

**Consensus recommendation:** Use 12-15x for most companies, adjust for quality

**4. Projection Period:**

| Period | Proponents | Rationale |
|--------|------------|-----------|
| 5 years | Some (Graham formula) | Shorter horizon, less uncertainty |
| 10 years | Most presenters (Sven Carlin, New Money, etc.) | Covers one business cycle |
| 15-20 years | Some academic approaches | Longer term view |

**Consensus recommendation:** 10 years (balances practicality with business cycle coverage)

### 3.3 Calculation Frequency Differences

**Year-by-Year Projection vs. Simple Growth:**

- **Detailed (Sven Carlin, Brent Coleman, Investing with Tom):** Project each year individually, can vary growth rates
- **Simplified (InvestingPH, Invest with Wesley):** Apply single growth rate over entire period
- **Two-Period (Investing with Tom):** Different rates for Years 1-5 vs. Years 6-10

### 3.4 Margin of Safety Preferences

| Margin Size | Proponents | When Used |
|-------------|------------|-----------|
| 10-20% | More confident/experienced investors | High certainty in business |
| 30% | InvestingPH, Many presenters | Standard for most situations |
| 50% | Phil Town (referenced by multiple), very conservative | New investors or uncertain situations |

**Consensus:** Use 30% as standard, adjust based on confidence and business predictability

## Part 4: The Distilled Best Approach

### 4.1 Recommended Methodology

**Hybrid Approach: Combining Best Practices**

Based on the analysis, the recommended approach is a **simplified DCF model** that balances:
- The rigor of full DCF
- The simplicity of Graham-like formulas
- The practicality of P/E-growth methods

### 4.2 The Six-Step Distilled Process

#### Step 1: Determine Current Free Cash Flow

**Definition:** Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures

**Where to Find:**
- Company's cash flow statement (annual report)
- Financial websites (Yahoo Finance, MSN Money, Simply Wall St)
- Look for: "Free Cash Flow" or calculate from components

**Important Adjustments (from Investing with Tom):**
1. **Stock-Based Compensation:** If disclosed, subtract from FCF
   - Real expense for shareholders
   - Accounts for dilution from share issuances
   
2. **Choose Representative Year:** Avoid outliers
   - Don't pick unusually high or low years
   - Use typical year based on research
   - If FCF is volatile, consider 3-year average

**Verification:**
- Cross-check from multiple sources
- Company reports are gold standard
- Financial websites can have errors

#### Step 2: Estimate Growth Rate

**Three-Tier Analysis:**

**Tier 1: Historical Performance (The Foundation)**
- Look at 10-year historical data
- Examine multiple metrics:
  - Earnings per share (EPS) growth
  - Revenue growth
  - Free cash flow growth
  - Equity growth (book value)
- Calculate CAGR (Compounded Annual Growth Rate)
  - Formula: (End Value ÷ Start Value)^(1/years) - 1
  - Use tools: Excel RATE function, calculator.net

**Tier 2: Industry and Competitive Analysis**
- Industry average growth rates
- Company's competitive position
- Market trends affecting future growth
- Management's guidance (if reliable)

**Tier 3: Conservative Adjustment (The Safety Margin)**
- Reduce historical rate by 20-40%
- Account for:
  - Law of large numbers (companies slow as they grow)
  - Increased competition
  - Market saturation
  - Unforeseeable challenges

**Two-Period Growth Structure (Best Practice from Investing with Tom):**
- **Years 1-5:** Use slightly higher growth rate (e.g., 15%)
  - More confidence in nearer term
  - Based on recent momentum and analyst estimates
  
- **Years 6-10:** Use more conservative rate (e.g., 8-10%)
  - Less confidence in distant future
  - Accounts for maturation
  - Provides margin of safety

**Example Calculation:**
- Historical CAGR: 18%
- Conservative adjustment: 20-30% reduction
- Years 1-5: 14% growth
- Years 6-10: 8% growth

#### Step 3: Project Future Cash Flows

**The Process:**

1. **Year 0:** Current adjusted free cash flow

2. **Years 1-10:** Apply growth rates
   - Year 1 = Year 0 × (1 + growth_rate_1-5)
   - Continue for years 1-5
   - Years 6-10 = Year 5 × (1 + growth_rate_6-10)
   - Continue for years 6-10

3. **Use Excel/Google Sheets:**
   - Lock growth rate cells (F4 key)
   - Drag formulas across years
   - Automate calculations

**Result:** 10-year projection of free cash flows

#### Step 4: Calculate Terminal Value

**Concept:** Value of business if sold at end of Year 10

**Calculation Method:** Terminal Value = Year 10 FCF × Multiple

**Multiple Selection Guidelines:**

| Business Quality | Suggested Multiple | Considerations |
|------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Poor/Average | 10-12x | Weak competitive position, declining industry |
| Good | 13-15x | Solid business, average competitive advantages |
| High Quality | 16-18x | Strong moats, durable competitive advantages |
| Exceptional | 19-22x+ | Wide moats, premium brands, high returns on capital |

**Factors to Consider:**
- Historical P/E or P/FCF range
- Industry norms
- Business quality and stability
- Competitive advantages (moats)
- Historical multiple trends (can use MacroTrends or similar)

**Conservative Approach:** Use lower end of appropriate range

#### Step 5: Discount to Present Value

**Step 5a: Select Discount Rate**

**Recommended Approach:** Use 10% consistently for all companies

**Rationale:**
- Charlie Munger mentioned Buffett targets 8-10% returns
- Close to S&P 500 historical average (10-12%)
- Provides reasonable required return
- Enables fair comparison across investments
- Simplicity over theoretical complexity

**Alternative (Higher Return Requirement):**
- If you want higher returns, use 12-15%
- Results in lower intrinsic values (more conservative)
- Must apply consistently

**Step 5b: Discount Cash Flows**

**Method 1: Individual Year Discounting (Most Accurate)**
- For each year n: PV = FCF_n ÷ (1 + discount_rate)^n
- Discount all 10 years individually
- Discount terminal value separately: PV_TV = TV ÷ (1 + discount_rate)^10

**Method 2: NPV Function (Excel Shortcut)**
- Formula: `=NPV(discount_rate, range_of_cash_flows)`
- Then discount terminal value separately
- Combine results

**Visual Check:**
- Discounted values should decrease as years increase
- Year 10 discounted value will be much smaller than Year 1
- This demonstrates time value of money

#### Step 6: Adjust for Net Cash/Debt

**Purpose:** Account for balance sheet value you're acquiring

**Calculation:**
- Net Cash/Debt = (Cash + Marketable Securities) - Total Debt
- If positive (net cash): Add to intrinsic value
- If negative (net debt): Subtract from intrinsic value

**Where to Find:**
- Company balance sheet
- Financial website statistics section
- Look for: "Total Cash" and "Total Debt"

**Importance:**
- Particularly critical for highly leveraged companies
- Significant net debt can dramatically reduce intrinsic value
- Significant net cash can provide buffer

#### Step 7: Calculate Per-Share Intrinsic Value

**Final Step:** Total Intrinsic Value ÷ Shares Outstanding

**Where to Find Shares Outstanding:**
- Company investor relations page (most accurate)
- Financial websites (may have errors)
- Look for: "Shares Outstanding - Diluted"

**Verification:**
- Check from multiple sources
- For companies with multiple share classes, sum all classes
- Critical to get this number correct

**Result:** Intrinsic value per share

## Part 5: Margin of Safety Application

### 5.1 The Concept

**Why It's Necessary:**
- All intrinsic value calculations are estimates
- Future is unpredictable
- Investors make mistakes
- Provides buffer against errors

**The Philosophy:** "Better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price" (Buffett)

### 5.2 Applying the Margin of Safety

**Calculation:**
- Buy Price = Intrinsic Value Per Share × (1 - Margin_of_Safety)

**Margin Sizes and When to Use:**

| Margin | Situation | Confidence Level |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| 50% | New investor, uncertain business, volatile industry | Low |
| 40% | Challenging business, some uncertainty | Low-Medium |
| 30% | **Standard for most situations** | Medium |
| 20% | Well-understood business, high confidence | High |
| 10% | Exceptional understanding, very high confidence | Very High |

**Practical Considerations:**
- 50% margins are very difficult to find in normal markets
- 30% provides good balance of safety and opportunity availability
- Experienced investors may use smaller margins for businesses they know exceptionally well
- Never buy at or above intrinsic value without margin

### 5.3 The Buy Decision

**Decision Framework:**

| Current Price vs. Buy Price | Action |
|------------------------------|--------|
| Price < Buy Price | Consider buying (if qualitative analysis passes) |
| Price ≈ Buy Price | Watch, don't buy yet |
| Price > Buy Price | Overvalued - avoid or wait |

**Watch List Strategy:**
1. Identify quality businesses you understand
2. Calculate intrinsic values and buy prices
3. Add to watch list
4. Wait patiently for market to reach buy price
5. Execute when opportunity presents

## Part 6: Essential Pre-Analysis Requirements

### 6.1 Qualitative Analysis (Do Before Valuation)

**Universal Consensus:** Never value a business you don't understand

**Required Analysis:**

1. **Business Model Understanding**
   - How does the company make money?
   - Products/services provided
   - Revenue streams
   - Customer base

2. **Competitive Advantages (Moats)**
   - Brand strength
   - Switching costs
   - Network effects
   - Cost advantages
   - Regulatory advantages

3. **Management Quality**
   - Track record
   - Capital allocation decisions
   - Shareholder alignment
   - Integrity and transparency

4. **Financial Health**
   - Debt levels and trend
   - Cash flow stability
   - Return on capital
   - Profit margins

5. **Industry Analysis**
   - Industry growth trends
   - Competitive landscape
   - Regulatory environment
   - Barriers to entry

**The Rule:** If the business doesn't pass qualitative analysis, don't bother calculating intrinsic value

### 6.2 The "Too Hard" Pile

**Buffett's Principle:** 95% of businesses are too hard to value

**When to Put in "Too Hard" Pile:**
- You don't understand the business model
- Cash flows are highly unpredictable
- Rapidly changing industry
- Complex financial structures
- High uncertainty about future
- Lack of historical data (startups, early-stage)

**The Benefit:** Focusing on businesses you can value leads to better results than trying to value everything

## Part 7: Scenario Analysis (Advanced Technique)

### 7.1 Why Use Scenarios

**Purpose:** Embrace uncertainty rather than pretend it doesn't exist

**Benefits:**
- Provides range of possible outcomes
- Mental preparation for different scenarios
- More robust decision-making
- Reduces overconfidence

### 7.2 Three-Scenario Framework (Sven Carlin's Approach)

**Scenario 1: Worst Case**
- Conservative growth rates (e.g., 0-5%)
- Lower terminal multiple (e.g., 10-12x)
- Probability: 20-25%
- Question: Can I tolerate this outcome?

**Scenario 2: Normal Case**
- Realistic growth rates (your best estimate)
- Appropriate terminal multiple
- Probability: 50-60%
- Most likely outcome

**Scenario 3: Best Case**
- Optimistic growth rates (e.g., 15-20%+)
- Higher terminal multiple (e.g., 18-22x)
- Probability: 20-25%
- Question: Would I be very happy with this outcome?

### 7.3 Probability-Weighted Intrinsic Value

**Calculation:**
- Intrinsic Value = (Worst × Probability) + (Normal × Probability) + (Best × Probability)

**Example:**
- Worst: $80 (25% probability)
- Normal: $120 (50% probability)
- Best: $160 (25% probability)
- Weighted IV = ($80 × 0.25) + ($120 × 0.50) + ($160 × 0.25) = $120

**Then Apply Margin of Safety to Weighted IV**

### 7.4 Risk/Reward Ratio

**Calculation:**
- Risk/Reward = Intrinsic Value ÷ Current Price

**Interpretation:**
- > 2.0: Excellent opportunity
- 1.5-2.0: Good opportunity
- 1.0-1.5: Marginal
- < 1.0: Avoid

## Part 8: Practical Implementation Tips

### 8.1 Building Your Model

**Excel/Google Sheets Template Structure:**

**Sheet 1: Assumptions**
- Current free cash flow
- Growth rates (Years 1-5, Years 6-10)
- Discount rate
- Terminal multiple
- Stock-based compensation
- Net cash/debt
- Shares outstanding
- Margin of safety

**Sheet 2: Cash Flow Projections**
- Year-by-year FCF (0-10)
- Terminal value calculation
- Discounting
- Present values

**Sheet 3: Intrinsic Value Summary**
- Total present value
- Net cash/debt adjustment
- Total intrinsic value
- Per share intrinsic value
- Buy price with margin of safety
- Comparison to current price
- Risk/reward ratio

### 8.2 Data Sources

**Free Sources:**
- Company investor relations websites (most accurate)
- Yahoo Finance, MSN Money
- SEC filings (EDGAR)
- Morningstar (basic)
- Simply Wall St (limited free tier)

**Paid Sources (for more serious analysis):**
- Gurufocus (historical metrics)
- Morningstar Premium
- Value Line
- Bloomberg Terminal

**What to Prioritize:**
- Company reports are gold standard
- Cross-check from multiple sources
- Verify unusual numbers

### 8.3 Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Overly Optimistic Growth Rates**
   - Solution: Always be conservative
   - Reduce historical rates

2. **Ignoring Time Value of Money**
   - Solution: Always discount future cash flows
   - Understand the impact

3. **Forgetting Terminal Value**
   - Solution: Include in every valuation
   - Use conservative multiples

4. **Not Applying Margin of Safety**
   - Solution: Always use 30% minimum
   - Never buy at intrinsic value

5. **Using Inconsistent Discount Rates**
   - Solution: Use same rate for all valuations
   - Recommended: 10%

6. **Valuing Businesses You Don't Understand**
   - Solution: Put in "too hard" pile
   - Focus on what you can value

7. **Ignoring Net Debt**
   - Solution: Always adjust for balance sheet
   - Critical for leveraged companies

8. **Single-Point Estimates**
   - Solution: Use scenario analysis
   - Understand range of outcomes

9. **Over-Reliance on Formulas**
   - Solution: Understand underlying business
   - Formulas are tools, not substitutes for analysis

10. **Not Updating Assumptions**
    - Solution: Regularly review and update
    - Market conditions and businesses change

## Part 9: Sensitivity Analysis

### 9.1 Why It Matters

**Purpose:** Understand how changes in assumptions affect valuation

**Key Insight:** Small changes in growth rate can dramatically change intrinsic value

### 9.2 Sensitivity Matrix

**Create a table showing intrinsic value at different growth rates:**

| Growth Rate | Intrinsic Value | Change from Base |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 5% | $X | -Y% |
| 10% | $Y | (Base) |
| 15% | $Z | +W% |

**Analyze:**
- Which growth rates are realistic?
- How sensitive is valuation to growth?
- Does the valuation hold across reasonable range?

### 9.3 Terminal Multiple Sensitivity

**Test different multiples:**

| Terminal Multiple | Intrinsic Value | Change from Base |
|------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 10x | $X | -Y% |
| 15x | $Y | (Base) |
| 20x | $Z | +W% |

**Understand:** Multiple choice significantly impacts valuation

### 9.4 Discount Rate Sensitivity

**Test different discount rates:**

| Discount Rate | Intrinsic Value | Change from Base |
|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 8% | $Z | +X% |
| 10% | $Y | (Base) |
| 12% | $X | -Y% |

**Note:** This is why consistency matters - use same rate for all valuations

### 9.5 Using Sensitivity Analysis

**Decision-Making Support:**
- If valuation holds across reasonable range: Higher confidence
- If valuation is highly sensitive: Lower confidence, require larger margin
- Helps identify key assumptions
- Informs conviction level in investment thesis

## Part 10: Complete Decision Framework

### 10.1 The Full Process

**Phase 1: Initial Screening**
1. Identify potential businesses
2. Quick qualitative analysis
3. Put businesses you don't understand in "too hard" pile
4. Proceed only with businesses you understand

**Phase 2: Qualitative Deep Dive**
1. Business model analysis
2. Competitive advantages assessment
3. Management evaluation
4. Financial health check
5. Industry analysis
6. **Decision:** Proceed to valuation only if business passes

**Phase 3: Quantitative Valuation**
1. Calculate current free cash flow
2. Determine growth rates (conservative)
3. Project future cash flows
4. Calculate terminal value
5. Discount to present value
6. Adjust for net cash/debt
7. Calculate per-share intrinsic value
8. Perform sensitivity analysis
9. Create scenario analysis (optional but recommended)

**Phase 4: Margin of Safety**
1. Apply 30% margin of safety (or appropriate for situation)
2. Calculate buy price
3. Compare to current market price
4. Calculate risk/reward ratio

**Phase 5: Investment Decision**
1. If price > buy price: Add to watch list, wait
2. If price ≈ buy price: Watch closely
3. If price < buy price: Consider buying (assuming qualitative passed)

### 10.2 Watch List Management

**Process:**
1. Maintain watch list of 20-30 quality businesses
2. Update valuations quarterly
3. Monitor for price movements
4. Act when price reaches buy level
5. Review assumptions regularly
6. Remove businesses that no longer meet criteria

**The Reality:**
- You might find 1-2 opportunities per year from 20-30 stock watch list
- This is sufficient for excellent returns
- Patience is more important than activity

## Part 11: Advanced Considerations

### 11.1 When DCF May Not Work Well

**Avoid or Be Cautious With:**
- Highly cyclical businesses
- Companies undergoing major transitions
- Startups and early-stage companies
- Businesses with negative cash flows
- Companies with unpredictable cash flows
- Rapidly changing industries

**Alternatives:**
- Put in "too hard" pile
- Use relative valuation (comparison to peers)
- Use asset-based valuation (build-up method)
- Wait until business stabilizes

### 11.2 Understanding the Limitations

**Inherent Limitations:**
- Future is unknowable
- Businesses change over time
- Unforeseeable events occur
- Human error in analysis
- Model doesn't capture everything

**The Right Mindset:**
- Intrinsic value is a tool, not a crystal ball
- Provides framework for decision-making
- Must be combined with judgment
- Embracing uncertainty is essential

### 11.3 Continuous Improvement

**Ways to Improve Over Time:**
- Track your valuations and actual outcomes
- Learn from mistakes
- Refine assumptions based on experience
- Study valuation successes and failures
- Read annual reports of great investors
- Practice valuations regularly

**Key Learning:**
- Consistency matters more than precision
- Process matters more than individual valuations
- Experience improves judgment on growth rates and multiples
- Understanding business quality improves with practice

## Part 12: Summary: The Distilled Approach

### 12.1 Core Principles (Non-Negotiable)

1. **Understand the Business First**
   - Never value what you don't understand
   - Qualitative analysis precedes quantitative

2. **Be Conservative**
   - Reduce historical growth rates
   - Use conservative multiples
   - Apply margin of safety

3. **Be Consistent**
   - Same methodology for all valuations
   - Same discount rate (10%)
   - Comparable assumptions

4. **Embrace Uncertainty**
   - Intrinsic value is a range, not exact number
   - Use scenario analysis
   - Apply margin of safety

5. **Be Patient**
   - Wait for opportunities
   - Maintain watch lists
   - Don't chase stocks

### 12.2 The Six-Step Process (Quick Reference)

1. **Current FCF:** Operating cash flow - CapEx, adjust for stock-based comp
2. **Growth Rate:** Historical CAGR reduced by 20-40%, two-period structure
3. **Project FCF:** Apply growth rates for 10 years
4. **Terminal Value:** Year 10 FCF × appropriate multiple (12-18x)
5. **Discount:** 10% discount rate, sum present values
6. **Adjust:** Net cash/debt, divide by shares, apply 30% margin of safety

### 12.3 The Decision Rule

**Buy When:**
- You understand the business well
- Business quality is good or better
- Current price < Buy Price (30% below intrinsic value)
- You can tolerate the worst-case scenario

**Don't Buy When:**
- You don't understand the business
- Price is at or above intrinsic value
- Business quality is poor
- Worst-case scenario is unacceptable

### 12.4 Final Wisdom

**From Warren Buffett (universally cited):**
"Intrinsic value is the present value of all the cash that a business will generate from now to Judgment Day, discounted at the proper discount rate."

**The Three Questions (Always Ask):**
1. How much cash will the business return?
2. When will I receive it?
3. How certain am I?

**The Ultimate Principle:**
"It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price."

**The Goal:**
Be approximately right rather than precisely wrong.

## Appendix: Key Formulas

### A1. Free Cash Flow
```
FCF = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures - Stock-Based Compensation
```

### A2. CAGR (Compounded Annual Growth Rate)
```
CAGR = (End Value ÷ Start Value)^(1 ÷ years) - 1
Excel: =RATE(years, 0, -start_value, end_value)
```

### A3. Future Cash Flow Projection
```
Year n FCF = Year (n-1) FCF × (1 + growth_rate)
```

### A4. Present Value
```
PV = FCF_n ÷ (1 + discount_rate)^n
```

### A5. Terminal Value
```
TV = Year 10 FCF × Terminal Multiple
```

### A6. Net Cash/Debt Adjustment
```
Net Cash = Total Cash + Marketable Securities - Total Debt
Adjusted Intrinsic Value = Total PV + Net Cash
```

### A7. Per Share Value
```
Intrinsic Value Per Share = Adjusted Intrinsic Value ÷ Shares Outstanding
```

### A8. Buy Price with Margin of Safety
```
Buy Price = Intrinsic Value Per Share × (1 - Margin_of_Safety)
```

### A9. Risk/Reward Ratio
```
Risk/Reward = Intrinsic Value Per Share ÷ Current Price
```

### A10. Benjamin Graham Formula (Alternative Method)
```
IV = EPS × [8.5 + (2 × G)] × 4.4 ÷ Y
Where:
EPS = Earnings Per Share
G = Expected growth rate (next 5 years)
Y = Current AAA corporate bond yield
```

## Glossary

**Buffett's "Too Hard" Pile:** Businesses that are too complex or unpredictable to value with confidence

**Cash Flow Statement:** Financial statement showing cash inflows and outflows

**CAGR:** Compounded Annual Growth Rate - smooths growth over time periods

**DCF:** Discounted Cash Flow - valuation method based on future cash flows

**Discount Rate:** Rate used to convert future values to present value

**Free Cash Flow (FCF):** Cash available for distribution to shareholders

**Graham Formula:** Benjamin Graham's simplified intrinsic value formula

**Intrinsic Value:** The true worth of a business based on its cash-generating ability

**Margin of Safety:** Buying price significantly below intrinsic value for protection

**Owner's Earnings:** Cash available to owners (Buffett's preferred term)

**Terminal Value:** Value of business beyond projection period

**WACC:** Weighted Average Cost of Capital - theoretical discount rate method (rarely used in practice)
